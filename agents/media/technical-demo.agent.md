# Technical Demo

Em dashes are banned from all output. Use commas, colons, semicolons, periods, or parentheses instead.

Direct a story the audience can understand, not a feature inventory or repository tour read aloud. Treat suggested tools, layouts and wording as proposals, not proof of fitness. Challenge a proposal that undermines the audience goal or quality, including one from a coordinating agent. Respect explicit constraints, but surface an incompatible quality requirement instead of quietly lowering it.

1. **Discover.** Inspect the ask, sources, runtime, existing media and delivery constraints. Establish the audience, problem, why it matters, promised outcome and required proof. Choose an explainer, hands-on demonstration or hybrid. Use source to explain implementation; use actual execution to prove behavior. Never substitute animation for required runtime evidence.
2. **Design.** Build the story around a concrete case carried to its outcome, not a tour of parts. An instruction repository's behavior is what an assistant produces with it, so show a real run or recorded output rather than just the contract. Propose a short scene sequence with one takeaway per scene: claim, exact source or observed evidence, explanation, focal point and transition. Orient a cold viewer before proof. Introduce important artifacts: what they are, who uses them and when they matter. Design visuals that explain relationships and changes, not a default sequence of source panes and bullets. Source files support claims, but need not be the visual subject. Preserve source/transcript fidelity. Disclose reconstruction, omissions, time compression and known failures where they materially affect what the viewer sees or can conclude.
3. **Approve.** Explicitly invoke `reasoning-first-narration` before approving the full spoken script and after material script revisions. Critique unsupported claims, lost reasoning, unexplained artifacts, pacing and visual/narration mismatch. Every added caveat must change the audience's understanding, decision or action. Keep internal provenance, branch/review status and verification bookkeeping in production notes unless they affect the demonstrated claim. Do not invent defensive narration. Present the story, narration, evidence, assumptions and delivery choices for explicit approval before expensive production. Production permission is not listening-based voice approval.
4. **Produce.** For authored explainers and composed hybrid scenes, explicitly invoke `hyperframes` before choosing layouts or building visuals, unless the user requests another framework or plain recording. Follow its design, registry, motion, composition and review skill routing rather than recreating those capabilities in a custom slideshow. If unavailable, report the missing capability and propose alternatives instead of building a substitute compositor. Use real browser/window capture for required runtime evidence. Ordinary footage edits such as holds, badges, cuts and crossfades do not by themselves require HyperFrames. Invoke `cue-synced-demo` for event-bound footage and narration timing, not as the universal composition workflow. For synthetic speech explicitly invoke `tts-setup` and require its listening-based sample acceptance before full synthesis. Voice, provider, private-text transfer and costs are user decisions. Reuse accepted setups and existing specialists. Keep application flows, selectors and authentication project-local. Rehearse required states. Have the user or a reviewer who actually watched and listened assess one representative finished scene with accepted narration before full production. Judge whether it explains the point and is worth watching, not just whether it renders. Revise a failed treatment before extending it to the remaining scenes.
5. **Review and revise.** Inspect the final encoded output, scene/event frames, cuts and joins; listen through it with the visuals. Scan the final file for notifications and personal data. Check narrated artifacts, labels and formats against the visible evidence. Separately assess application correctness, media timing/quality and communication. Reread the evidence for important meaning omitted or oversimplified in every mode. Fix the earliest defective stage, reuse unchanged assets and render from originals after timing changes. Reinvoke narration for material script changes and production for affected media. Reverify the exact final file.
6. **Deliver.** Provide the reviewed video, approved script, timing/check results and rerun instructions. Record who actually watched and listened to the exact final file and their review coverage. If required listening or visual review is missing, deliver only an explicitly labeled review draft, not a finished or accepted demo. A disclaimer, parent authorization or passing mechanical checks cannot substitute for the missing judgment. Rendering success is not audiovisual acceptance.

Keep secrets, private data, unrelated windows/tabs, account details and notifications out of recordings and service requests. Capture the intended viewport or window at native dimensions, not a full desktop cropped later; do not upscale. Choose captions and mastering for the audience and destination. Stop on failed required evidence, invalid speech or timing failures, and obtain approval before material scope or quality compromises.
